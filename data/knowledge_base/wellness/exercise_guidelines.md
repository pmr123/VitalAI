# Exercise Guidelines for Health

## Official Recommendations

### Adults (18-64 years) - WHO/CDC Guidelines
**Minimum weekly activity:**
- 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, OR
- 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity, OR
- Equivalent combination of both
- PLUS muscle-strengthening activities 2+ days per week

**For additional health benefits:**
- 300+ minutes of moderate activity, OR
- 150+ minutes of vigorous activity

### Older Adults (65+)
- Same as adults, with emphasis on balance training
- Activities to improve flexibility
- Modify intensity based on fitness level and health conditions

## Types of Exercise

### Aerobic (Cardio) Exercise
**Moderate intensity examples:**
- Brisk walking (3-4 mph)
- Water aerobics
- Cycling (<10 mph)
- Tennis (doubles)
- Ballroom dancing
- Gardening

**Vigorous intensity examples:**
- Running/jogging
- Swimming laps
- Cycling (>10 mph)
- Tennis (singles)
- Hiking uphill
- Jump rope
- HIIT workouts

**How to tell the difference:**
- Moderate: Can talk but not sing
- Vigorous: Can only say a few words before pausing for breath

### Strength Training
**Benefits:**
- Builds and maintains muscle mass
- Strengthens bones
- Improves metabolism
- Enhances functional fitness

**Guidelines:**
- 2+ days per week
- All major muscle groups
- 8-12 repetitions per exercise
- 2-3 sets per muscle group

**Examples:**
- Weight lifting
- Resistance bands
- Bodyweight exercises (push-ups, squats)
- Yoga (certain types)

### Flexibility and Balance
**Benefits:**
- Prevents injuries
- Improves range of motion
- Reduces fall risk (especially important for older adults)

**Examples:**
- Stretching
- Yoga
- Tai chi
- Pilates

## Heart Rate Training Zones

Based on Maximum Heart Rate (MHR ≈ 220 - age):

| Zone | % of MHR | Feel | Duration | Benefit |
|------|----------|------|----------|---------|
| 1 | 50-60% | Very light | Long | Warm-up, recovery |
| 2 | 60-70% | Light | 30-60 min | Fat burning, base fitness |
| 3 | 70-80% | Moderate | 20-40 min | Aerobic capacity |
| 4 | 80-90% | Hard | 10-20 min | Speed, performance |
| 5 | 90-100% | Maximum | 1-5 min | Peak performance |

**Example for 40-year-old (MHR = 180):**
- Zone 2: 108-126 BPM
- Zone 3: 126-144 BPM
- Zone 4: 144-162 BPM

## Starting an Exercise Program

### If You're Currently Inactive
**Week 1-2:**
- 10-15 minutes walking daily
- Light stretching

**Week 3-4:**
- 20-25 minutes walking
- Add simple bodyweight exercises

**Week 5-8:**
- 30 minutes moderate activity
- 2 days strength training

**After 8 weeks:**
- Gradually increase intensity and duration
- Try new activities

### Progression Principles
1. **Start slow** - Begin below your capacity
2. **Increase gradually** - No more than 10% increase per week
3. **Listen to your body** - Rest when needed
4. **Stay consistent** - Regular exercise beats sporadic intense workouts
5. **Include variety** - Mix cardio, strength, and flexibility

## Exercise and Wearable Metrics

### What Your Wearable Tracks
- **Heart rate** during exercise
- **Active minutes** in moderate+ intensity
- **Calories burned** during activity
- **Steps** throughout the day
- **Recovery** metrics post-exercise

### Using Data Effectively
- Track exercise heart rate to ensure appropriate intensity
- Monitor weekly active minutes toward goals
- Use recovery metrics to avoid overtraining
- Notice trends over time

## When to Exercise

### Best Times
- **Morning:** May improve sleep, easier to maintain habit
- **Afternoon:** Body temperature peaks (potentially better performance)
- **Evening:** Can relieve stress, but may affect sleep if too late

### Avoid
- Vigorous exercise within 2-3 hours of bedtime
- Exercise when exhausted or sick
- Training same muscle groups on consecutive days

## Safety Considerations

### Warning Signs During Exercise
Stop and seek help if you experience:
- Chest pain or pressure
- Severe shortness of breath
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Irregular heartbeat
- Nausea or vomiting
- Pain in arms, neck, or jaw

### Exercise with Health Conditions
Consult your doctor before starting if you have:
- Heart disease or high blood pressure
- Diabetes
- Joint or bone problems
- Recent surgery
- Chronic illness
- Pregnancy

### Hydration
- Drink water before, during, and after exercise
- ~500ml 2-3 hours before
- ~200ml every 15-20 minutes during
- Replace fluids lost after

## Making Exercise Stick

### Strategies for Consistency
1. **Schedule it** - Put in calendar like any appointment
2. **Start small** - Lower barrier to entry
3. **Find enjoyment** - You'll stick with what you like
4. **Get a buddy** - Accountability helps
5. **Track progress** - Wearables help motivate
6. **Reward yourself** - Celebrate milestones (non-food rewards)

### Overcoming Barriers
| Barrier | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| No time | 10-minute sessions, active commute |
| Too tired | Start with light activity, energy often increases |
| Weather | Indoor options, home workouts |
| Cost | Walking is free, bodyweight exercises |
| Don't know how | Apps, YouTube videos, classes |
