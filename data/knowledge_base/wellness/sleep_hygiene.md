# Sleep Hygiene: Guide to Better Sleep

## What is Sleep Hygiene?
Sleep hygiene refers to the habits, practices, and environmental factors that are conducive to sleeping well on a regular basis.

## Why Sleep Matters

### Health Benefits of Good Sleep
- Improved immune function
- Better weight management
- Reduced risk of heart disease
- Better mental health and mood
- Enhanced cognitive function and memory
- Lower inflammation
- Better physical performance
- Improved relationships and productivity

### Consequences of Poor Sleep
- Increased accident risk
- Weight gain
- Weakened immunity
- Heart disease risk
- Mental health issues (anxiety, depression)
- Cognitive impairment
- Reduced quality of life

## The Ideal Sleep Environment

### Temperature
- Optimal: 65-68°F (18-20°C)
- Cooler is generally better
- Body temperature naturally drops during sleep
- Too warm disrupts deep sleep

### Darkness
- Complete darkness is best
- Light suppresses melatonin production
- Use blackout curtains
- Cover LED lights on devices
- Consider an eye mask

### Sound
- Quiet is ideal
- White noise can mask disruptive sounds
- Earplugs for noisy environments
- Avoid falling asleep to TV

### Bedding
- Comfortable, supportive mattress
- Appropriate pillows for sleep position
- Clean, breathable bedding
- Replace mattress every 7-10 years

### Air Quality
- Good ventilation
- Consider air purifier if allergies
- Moderate humidity (30-50%)

## Pre-Sleep Routine

### 2-3 Hours Before Bed
- Finish eating (large meals)
- Complete vigorous exercise
- Limit fluids to avoid nighttime bathroom trips
- Avoid caffeine (ideally none after 2pm)

### 1-2 Hours Before Bed
- Dim lights in your home
- Begin winding down activities
- Avoid stressful tasks or conversations
- Light snack if hungry (complex carbs + protein)

### 30-60 Minutes Before Bed
- Stop screen use (phones, tablets, computers)
- Relaxing activities: reading, gentle stretching, bath
- Prepare for next day to reduce worry
- Lower room temperature

### At Bedtime
- Consistent time every night (including weekends)
- Relaxation techniques if needed
- Comfortable sleep clothes
- Dark, cool, quiet room

## Things to Avoid

### In the Evening
- Caffeine (coffee, tea, soda, chocolate)
- Alcohol (disrupts sleep cycles)
- Large meals
- Vigorous exercise
- Stressful activities
- Bright lights
- Screens (blue light)

### In Bed
- Watching TV
- Working
- Phone/tablet use
- Eating
- Worrying/planning
- Clock watching

## Establishing a Sleep Schedule

### Guidelines
1. **Same bedtime daily** - Even weekends
2. **Same wake time daily** - More important than bedtime
3. **No more than 1 hour variation** - On weekends
4. **Wake up with light** - Natural or light therapy

### If You Can't Sleep
- Get out of bed after 20 minutes
- Do something boring in dim light
- Return when sleepy
- Don't watch the clock
- Avoid screens

## Common Sleep Disruptors

### Caffeine
- Half-life of 5-6 hours
- Some people metabolize slower
- Hidden sources: chocolate, some medications, some teas
- Consider eliminating after noon

### Alcohol
- May help fall asleep initially
- Disrupts sleep cycles later in night
- Reduces REM sleep
- Causes more awakenings
- Worsens snoring/sleep apnea

### Screen Time
- Blue light suppresses melatonin
- Content can be stimulating
- Use night mode/blue light filters if must use
- Best to avoid 1+ hours before bed

### Stress and Worry
- Practice stress management
- Write down worries before bed
- Use relaxation techniques
- Consider therapy if chronic

## Relaxation Techniques for Sleep

### Deep Breathing
1. Breathe in slowly through nose (4 seconds)
2. Hold (4-7 seconds)
3. Exhale slowly through mouth (8 seconds)
4. Repeat 3-4 times

### Progressive Muscle Relaxation
1. Starting with toes, tense muscles for 5 seconds
2. Relax for 30 seconds
3. Move up through body (legs, abdomen, arms, face)
4. Notice the contrast between tension and relaxation

### Body Scan Meditation
1. Lie comfortably with eyes closed
2. Focus attention on each body part sequentially
3. Notice sensations without judgment
4. Progress from toes to head

### Visualization
1. Imagine a peaceful place
2. Engage all senses (sight, sound, smell, touch)
3. Stay in the scene mentally
4. Allow yourself to drift off

## When to Seek Help

Consult a healthcare provider if you:
- Consistently can't fall asleep within 30 minutes
- Wake frequently and can't return to sleep
- Wake too early unable to go back to sleep
- Don't feel refreshed despite adequate hours
- Snore loudly or stop breathing during sleep
- Have excessive daytime sleepiness
- Experience symptoms for more than a few weeks

### Possible Sleep Disorders
- Insomnia
- Sleep apnea
- Restless leg syndrome
- Narcolepsy
- Circadian rhythm disorders

## Sleep Tracking

### Benefits
- Identify patterns
- See effects of lifestyle changes
- Detect potential issues

### Limitations
- Consumer devices ~70-80% accurate
- Can cause "orthosomnia" (anxiety about sleep data)
- Clinical sleep studies more accurate for diagnosis

### Best Practices
- Use for trends, not absolute numbers
- Don't obsess over nightly variations
- Focus on how you feel, not just data
